Keezhadi (Keeladi) is an archaeological site near Madurai in Sivaganga district, excavated from 2015 onward by the Tamil Nadu State Department of Archaeology. It has drawn global attention for possible urban settlement in the Sangam period.

Excavators reported structures, pottery, beads, terracotta ring wells, and Tamil-Brahmi inscriptions. Some scholars associate phases with 6th century BCE to 1st century CE, suggesting organized town life in the Vaigai valley earlier than some textbooks assumed.

Artifacts include dice, spindle whorls, and craft tools hinting at commerce and industry. Debate continues over exact dates and links to Sangam Madurai, but the site strengthens evidence for complex society in ancient Tamilakam.

Keezhadi museum displays findings for public education. For students, Keezhadi illustrates how archaeology complements literary Sangam sources—not replacing poems, but grounding them in material life.

The excavations sparked public interest in Tamil heritage and discussions about urbanization, trade, and writing systems in South India before large empires unified the region.

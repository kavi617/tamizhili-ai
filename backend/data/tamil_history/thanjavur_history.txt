Thanjavur (Tanjore) in the Kaveri delta became the Chola imperial capital under Rajaraja I and Rajendra I. Its fertile soil supported rice surpluses that funded armies, temples, and poets.

The Brihadeeswarar Temple dominates the skyline; the city also holds the Saraswathi Mahal Library with palm-leaf manuscripts, Nayaka palace remains, and Maratha-era court history after Chola decline.

Thanjavur painting (Tanjore art), classical music (Carnatic tradition links to Tyagaraja festival region), and bronze casting workshops sustain cultural economy.

British-era annexation integrated Thanjavur into Madras Presidency; post-independence it remains an agricultural and educational hub. Inscriptions across the district document Chola village assemblies and temple land rights.

Students studying Tamil history treat Thanjavur as a laboratory—temple archaeology, delta ecology, and living performance traditions in one cityscape shaped by a thousand years of Tamil statecraft.

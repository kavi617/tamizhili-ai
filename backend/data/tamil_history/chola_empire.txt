The Chola Empire (Tamil: சோழர் கட்டுப்பாடு) was one of the great Tamil dynasties of southern India. It rose to prominence in the early medieval period, with its heartland in the fertile Kaveri (Cauvery) delta of present-day Tamil Nadu.

Early Cholas are mentioned in Sangam literature. The medieval Chola revival began around the 9th century CE under Vijayalaya Chola, who captured Thanjavur. The empire reached its zenith under Rajaraja Chola I (985–1014 CE) and his son Rajendra Chola I (1014–1044 CE).

Rajaraja built the Brihadeeswarar Temple at Thanjavur, a UNESCO World Heritage monument and a masterpiece of Dravidian architecture. His reign saw administrative reforms, a strong navy, and expansion into Sri Lanka and the Maldives.

Rajendra Chola extended power north to the Ganges, taking the title “Gangaikonda Chola” after a celebrated campaign. He founded Gangaikonda Cholapuram and built another great temple there. Chola fleets controlled trade routes across the Bay of Bengal, reaching Southeast Asia.

Chola bronze sculpture—especially Nataraja (dancing Shiva) images—became world-famous for grace and technical skill. Inscriptions on temple walls record land grants, taxes, and local assemblies (sabha), showing a sophisticated agrarian and temple-centered economy.

The empire declined after the 13th century due to succession struggles and pressure from the Pandyas and Hoysalas, but Chola art, literature, and temple culture deeply shaped Tamil identity and remain central to Tamil heritage today.

The Chera Dynasty (Tamil: சேரர்) ruled the west and central parts of ancient Tamilakam, including the Malabar coast and the fertile lands around the Periyar and Pamba rivers. Cheras appear alongside Cholas and Pandyas in Sangam poetry as one of the three great Tamil crowns.

Chera ports such as Muziris (Muchiri) connected Tamilakam to the Roman and Mediterranean worlds. Pepper, pearls, ivory, and cotton left these harbors; gold coins, wine, and glass arrived in return. Sangam poems praise Chera warriors and kings who patronized bards.

Legend associates the Chera king Senguttuvan with the Pattini cult and military expeditions to the Himalayas in later tradition. Historical Chera power fluctuated across centuries, with capitals at places like Karur and later Mahodayapuram in the medieval period.

Chera contributions include trade networks, hill-country culture (kurinji landscapes in poetry), and integration of Tamil speakers along the western ghats. After the Sangam age, Chera lines merged into complex regional politics with Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas, and later the Zamorin of Calicut in Kerala history.

The fish-and-bow Chera emblem appears in Sangam anthologies, reminding readers that Tamil political identity was never a single kingdom but a tapestry of rival yet related dynasties.

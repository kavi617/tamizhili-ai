Ancient Tamilakam (Tamil: தமிழகம்) connected to the world through ports on both coasts. Sangam poems name Puhar (Kaveripoompattinam), Muziris, and Korkai among busy harbors.

Merchants exported pepper, pearls, ivory, cardamom, and fine cotton; imports included gold, wine, glass, and horses. Yavanas (foreigners) appear in poetry and archaeology (Roman pottery sherds).

The monsoon system enabled seasonal sailing to the Red Sea and Southeast Asia. Tamil merchant cooperatives and guilds (manigramam, nagaram) appear in later inscriptions, showing institutional trade memory.

Coastal shrines blessed voyages; kings taxed ports and protected shipping when strong. Loss of port silting and political chaos sometimes shifted trade to new harbors.

Understanding Tamil ports reframes history beyond inland temples—Tamil society was ocean-facing, multilingual at the waterfront, and economically tied to Rome, Sri Lanka, and insular Southeast Asia for centuries.

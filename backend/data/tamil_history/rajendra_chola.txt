Rajendra Chola I (reigned c. 1014–1044 CE), son of Rajaraja I, pushed Chola expansion further. He took the title Gangaikonda Chola (“Chola who took the Ganges”) after a celebrated northern campaign, founding Gangaikonda Cholapuram as a new capital near the Kaveri.

There he built another great Brihadeeswara-style temple, slightly shorter than Thanjavur’s but equally grand, with a vast palace complex and a deep well called Simhakeni. Inscriptions boast of victories and tribute.

Rajendra’s navy operated across the Bay of Bengal, reaching Southeast Asian ports. Chola influence appears in regional chronicles and art as far as parts of modern Indonesia and Malaysia, often through trade and ritual exchange rather than direct rule.

His inscriptions record gifts to temples, grants to Brahmins, and management of conquered territories. Succession disputes after his death contributed to later Chola strain, but his reign marks the peak of medieval Tamil sea power.

Students remember Rajendra for linking Tamil kingship to the sacred Ganges river in title and myth, symbolizing pan-Indian ambition from a Tamil base.

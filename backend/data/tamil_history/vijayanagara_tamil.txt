The Vijayanagara Empire (14th–17th centuries) ruled much of South India after Delhi Sultanate incursions weakened older Tamil dynasties. Though based near Hampi, its armies and governors shaped Tamil Nadu politics.

Tamil regions saw Nayaka governors at Madurai, Thanjavur, and Gingee build temples, tanks, and palaces. Nayaka architecture blended Vijayanagara pillars with Tamil gopurams—visible at Madurai and Rameswaram renovations.

Telugu and Tamil languages shared court patronage; inscriptions are multilingual. Trade in textiles and horses continued. Vijayanagara defeat at Talikota (1565) fragmented power, but Nayaka states persisted into the 17th century.

European traders (Portuguese, Dutch, English) increasingly appeared on Tamil coasts during this transition. Vijayanagara-era Tamil society shows bhakti devotion, temple festivals, and agrarian expansion under new warrior elites.

For Tamil history courses, Vijayanagara marks the bridge between medieval Chola-Pandya memory and early modern colonial contact—not a “Tamil dynasty” but a major force in Tamil country.

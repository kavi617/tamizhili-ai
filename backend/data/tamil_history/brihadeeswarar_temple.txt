The Brihadeeswarar Temple at Thanjavur (Tamil: பிரகதீஸ்வரர் கோயில்) is a Chola masterpiece built around 1010 CE under Rajaraja Chola I. Dedicated to Shiva as Brihadeeswara (“Great Lord”), it is also called the Big Temple (Periya Kovil).

The vimana (tower) rises about 66 meters, one of the tallest temple towers of its time, capped with a single granite kalasha weighing many tons—an engineering feat that still inspires debate among historians and engineers.

The temple complex includes a massive Nandi sculpture, mural paintings (Chola-era frescoes), and thousands of inscriptions detailing donations, land grants, dancers, musicians, and temple staff. It functioned as a religious, economic, and administrative hub.

UNESCO lists it as part of the Great Living Chola Temples World Heritage site. Its symmetry, scale, and bronze processional images exemplify Chola integration of ritual, art, and state power.

Visitors today see not only architecture but a living temple where daily puja continues, linking modern Tamil devotion to Rajaraja’s 11th-century vision of imperial Shiva worship.

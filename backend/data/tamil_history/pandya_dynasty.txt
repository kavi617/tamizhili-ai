The Pandya Dynasty (Tamil: பாண்டியர்) was among the three ancient Tamil royal lines—the Cheras, Cholas, and Pandyas—celebrated in Sangam poetry. Their traditional capital was Madurai, a city long associated with Tamil learning and the Meenakshi Amman Temple.

Sangam texts describe Pandyas as patrons of poets and warriors who ruled fertile lands fed by the Vaigai River. Legend links the Third Tamil Sangam to Madurai under Pandya patronage, where scholars compiled anthologies such as the Eight Anthologies (Ettuthokai) and the Ten Idylls (Pattuppattu).

In the early historic period, Pandyas traded with Rome and the Mediterranean world. Roman coins and pottery found in Tamil Nadu reflect active export of pearls, spices, and cotton. Madurai remained a commercial and cultural hub for centuries.

Medieval Pandyas revived in the 13th century after Chola decline. Marco Polo noted the wealth of the Pandya kingdom. The dynasty built and expanded major temples, including the grand gopurams (gateway towers) of the Meenakshi-Sundareswarar complex.

Pandya rulers often competed with Cholas and later with the Delhi Sultanate’s southern expeditions. The invasion of Malik Kafur in the early 14th century weakened the kingdom. Regional powers and the Vijayanagara Empire later absorbed much of their territory.

Despite political change, Pandya-era contributions to Tamil literature, temple architecture, and pearl fisheries left a lasting mark. The fish emblem of the Pandyas still appears in Tamil cultural symbols, recalling one of the oldest continuous royal traditions of the Tamil country.

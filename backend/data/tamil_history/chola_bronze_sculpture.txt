Chola bronze sculpture (Tamil: சோழர் வெண்கலம்) reached world fame for images of Shiva Nataraja (Lord of Dance), Devi, and saints cast in the lost-wax (cire perdue) method.

Workshops in Swamimalai and other towns continue traditions. Chola-period bronzes show balanced proportions, jewelry detail, and dynamic poses—especially Nataraja within a flame aureole (prabha).

Temples used bronzes for processions (utsvamurthis); stone deities remained fixed. Inscriptions record gifts of metal images and maintenance endowments.

Colonial-era collectors brought Cholas bronzes to museums worldwide; legally exported pieces remain contested heritage topics. Authentic Chola bronzes are dated by style, inscription, and metallurgical study.

The dancing Shiva symbolizes cosmic creation and destruction in Tamil Saiva thought. For art history, Chola bronze is a high point of Indian metal casting, rivaling any contemporary European medieval sculpture in technical skill.

Today, Tamil Nadu laws protect antique bronzes while allowing living artisans to supply new temple images under ritual rules.

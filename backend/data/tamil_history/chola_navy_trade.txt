The Chola navy (Tamil: சோழர் கடற்படை) made the Bay of Bengal a “Chola lake” in the 11th century. Fleets supported trade, diplomacy, and military expeditions to Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia.

Ships carried textiles, spices, gems, and metal goods. Tamil merchant groups (nagarams) organized long-distance exchange with ports in Sri Vijaya, Kadaram (Kedah), and beyond. Chola inscriptions mention naval expeditions and booty, but also stable trade routes.

Coastal temples and merchant guild inscriptions (e.g., Aihole-style Tamil merchant records at other sites) show how religion and commerce intertwined. Harbors on the Coromandel coast linked Kaveri delta agriculture to Indian Ocean networks.

The navy protected shipping from piracy and projected power during Rajendra I’s reign. Decline of Chola sea dominance followed 13th-century political fragmentation, yet Tamil fishing and trading communities maintained maritime skills.

Modern historians study Chola bronzes, Southeast Asian Chola-period remains, and Tamil inscriptions to reconstruct this Indian Ocean world—where Tamil was one language of prestige among many.

Madurai (Tamil: மதுரை) is among the oldest continuously inhabited cities in South India, celebrated in Sangam literature as the seat of Pandya power and the Third Tamil Sangam tradition.

The city lies on the Vaigai River, supporting agriculture and trade. Greek and Roman sources indirectly reference inland cities of the far south; Madurai’s fame grew through Tamil poetry before extensive stone archaeology.

The Nayaka period (16th–17th centuries) rebuilt the Meenakshi-Sundareswarar complex with towering gopurams. Madurai became a weaving and publishing center in colonial and modern times.

Political history includes Pandyas, Sultanate raids, Vijayanagara oversight, British annexation, and post-independence industry. Jallikattu protests and Tamil cultural movements often invoke Madurai as a symbol of Tamil identity.

Today Madurai blends pilgrimage tourism, universities, and IT corridors. For learners, Madurai links Sangam imagination, temple urbanism, and living Tamil speech (Madurai Tamil dialect).

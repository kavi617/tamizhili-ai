The Pallava Dynasty (Tamil: பல்லவர்) dominated parts of northern Tamilakam from roughly the 4th to 9th centuries CE, with strong centers at Kanchipuram and the coast at Mamallapuram (Mahabalipuram).

Pallava rulers such as Mahendravarman I and Narasimhavarman I were patrons of art, architecture, and religion. Mamallapuram’s rock-cut rathas, the Shore Temple, and massive bas-reliefs like “Arjuna’s Penance” show early Dravidian sculptural genius.

Pallavas competed with Chalukyas, Pandyas, and later Cholas. Their inscriptions record grants to Brahmins, temples, and irrigation tanks. Kanchipuram became a sacred city with major Shaiva and Vaishnava temples still visited today.

The Pallava script evolution influenced later Grantha and Tamil writing systems. Architecturally, they experimented with monolithic temples before the fully structural stone temples of the Chola zenith.

Pallava decline opened space for Chola revival under Vijayalaya. Yet their coastal monuments remain UNESCO World Heritage sites, proving how Tamil rulers used stone, myth, and port power to project culture across the Bay of Bengal.

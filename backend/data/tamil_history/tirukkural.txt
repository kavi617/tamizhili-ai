The Tirukkural (Tamil: திருக்குறள்) is a classic Tamil text of 1,330 couplets (kurals) on virtue (aram), wealth (porul), and love (inbam), attributed to the poet-sage Thiruvalluvar. Scholars often date its composition broadly to between 300 BCE and 500 CE, though debate continues.

Unlike Sangam love-war anthologies, the Kural offers concise ethical maxims usable in daily life and governance. It draws on Tamil, Jain, and possibly Buddhist ethical ideas without heavy sectarian ritual language.

Couplets address friendship, family, kingship, justice, and self-control. Section on “Arathupaal” (virtue) is taught in Tamil schools today. The work has been translated into dozens of languages.

The Valluvar statue in Kanyakumari and celebrations on Thiruvalluvar Day show modern reverence. For historians, the Kural proves a parallel Tamil moral literature alongside court poetry—aimed at all society, not only warriors and lovers.

The text remains a bridge between ancient Tamilakam and contemporary Tamil identity, cited by reformers, politicians, and teachers as a common ethical vocabulary.

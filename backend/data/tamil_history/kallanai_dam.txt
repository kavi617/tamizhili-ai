The Kallanai Dam (Grand Anicut) on the Kaveri River in Tamil Nadu is one of the oldest water-diversion structures still in use, traditionally attributed to Karikala Chola of the early Chola line (Sangam-era king celebrated in poetry).

The dam redirects Kaveri water into fertile delta channels, supporting rice cultivation that fed Chola cities and temples. British engineers under Sir Arthur Cotton studied and modified it in the 19th century while respecting the ancient core.

Irrigation made the Chola heartland among the most productive agrarian zones in India. Tank cascades, canal networks, and village assemblies (sabha) managed local water in inscriptions from later Chola periods.

Kallanai symbolizes Tamil engineering long before modern dams. Farmers, poets, and kings all depended on Kaveri regulation—linking environmental history to empire.

Visitors see stone masonry, river views, and plaques explaining Chola and colonial alterations. For தமிழி AI learners, Kallanai connects kingship, agriculture, and daily survival in Tamil history.

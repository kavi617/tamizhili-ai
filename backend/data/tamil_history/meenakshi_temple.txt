The Meenakshi Amman Temple in Madurai (Tamil: மீனாட்சி அம்மன் கோயில்) is a major center of Tamil Shaivism and city identity. The goddess Meenakshi (Parvati) and her consort Sundareswarar (Shiva) are the presiding deities.

Medieval and later Pandyas, Nayaks, and donors expanded the complex across centuries. Its towering gopurams (gateway towers) are covered with brightly painted stucco figures of gods, heroes, and mythical beings—one of the most recognizable skylines in Tamil Nadu.

The temple’s Thousand Pillar Hall, golden lotus tank, and annual festivals draw millions. Inscriptions and literary sources tie Madurai to Sangam assemblies and Pandya patronage of Tamil poetry.

The temple is not only religion but urban planning: streets around the shrine follow traditional concentric layouts described in agamic texts. Meenakshi symbolizes Tamil Shakti worship and the enduring link between Madurai and Tamil literary memory.

For students of history, the temple archives stone records of gifts, taxes, and guilds, showing how sacred space anchored economic life in pre-colonial South India.
